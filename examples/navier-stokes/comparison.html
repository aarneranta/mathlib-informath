The question

Lean has checked every proof of openai/NavierStokesAndEuler (no sorry, only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound). What Lean cannot check is whether the statements say what they are meant to say. There are two such questions:

  1. Is the main theorem the intended one? Do the theorem and the definitions it rests on state the Clay problem, Fefferman's alternatives (C) and (D)? If they do, the result is proved, whatever the proofs look like.
  2. Is the formalization the paper's argument? Do the intermediate lemmas say what the paper's lemmas say? This is what Navier–Stokes lost in translation doubts. It matters for trusting the paper, not the theorem.

4. Does symbolic informalization help?

On this evidence, yes, in three ways:

  1. The statement. The main theorem and its definitions read as a mathematician would write them (Section 1). Whether the result is what it claims to be can be checked by someone who does not read Lean, on about ten declarations instead of 641,000 lines.
  2. The deviations. Both of the article's examples are visible in the readings: $C^{n+5}$ against the paper's $C^{m+4}$, and the term with $A_R$ against (10.19). Readers who put the readings beside the paper's lemmas would find them without reading Lean. Because the readings are made by a grammar from the formal statement, a deviation cannot be smoothed over by a fluent paraphrase, which is the risk of an informalization by a language model.
  3. Whether it matters. The formal side answers questions that the text cannot: which lemmas the main theorem actually uses. The lemma the article prints is not used; the ones that are used are proved with the extra derivative and the extra term, and Lean has checked that the argument closes with them.

What it does not do:

  • Match lemmas to the paper. The Lean code does not say which declaration formalizes which lemma of the paper; the article had to find "the closest" one. The readings make the comparison possible, not automatic.
  • Judge the deviations. Whether $m + 5$ for $m + 4$ is harmless is a mathematical judgement. Informalization shows the deviation; it does not weigh it.
  • Vouch for the symbol table. A reading is as faithful as the symbol table it uses. The 85 entries of navier-stokes.dkgf were written for this sample, each from its Lean definition, which is quoted beside it in the table; they too must be checked.
