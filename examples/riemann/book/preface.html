Preface

This is an informalization of grh-lean, a Lean 4 proof of the Riemann hypothesis and of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. The proof is Levent Alpöge's, with Claude (remark.pdf); grh-lean is a shorter rewriting of his Lean proof, made at Anthropic with Claude, which follows its method and proves many steps by other arguments. It is built on Mathlib and uses only Lean's axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. grh-lean comes in two editions; this book reads the compact one (grh-compact, about 76,000 lines of Lean), and consults the readable one (grh) for explanations.

The book contains every declaration of the project that the five theorems of src/Main.lean depend on, and no others: the background results that Mathlib lacks (src/Lib) and the steps of the argument (src/New). They are given in an order where nothing is used before it is stated: each module after the modules it imports, and in each module the order of its source. The declarations are grouped in chapters by the folders of the source, one page each; a folder whose modules depend on other folders' appears again in a later chapter (“continued”).

Each declaration is shown with

  • its statement, as Informath reads it in English from the Lean statement; where Informath has no reading, Lean's own statement is shown;
  • the comment that the source gives it, if any, in a grey frame;
  • for a theorem, its proof in one line: the declarations of the project that the proof uses, each linked to its statement in the book, and, in a fold, the theorems of Mathlib that it applies, each linked to its place in Mathlib (not those that Mathlib's tactics apply on their own). The proofs of Mathlib's theorems are not shown;
  • its file and identifier in grh-lean, linked to the line of the source on GitHub.

As in grh-lean's guide, P is a parameter set and D an admissible system for P throughout, and the parts of D and P are written without them: x, m, a for D.x, D.m, D.a, d for P.d. The letters are those of the compact edition's guide (grh-compact/GUIDE.md, section 3): x, x₁, b are scales, m the number of theta lifts, k and r the ranks of columns and rows, a the end of the prime sums.

The statements are read by machine, by Informath with the symbol tables of mathlib-informath and a table of this project; nothing in them is written by hand. A reading says what the Lean statement says, in words where the tables have words for its notions and in formulas otherwise; it can be long, and some notions of the project are shown by their Lean names. The proofs are not read: the book only says what each proof relies on. To check that the theorems hold, one checks the Lean proof itself, as the README of grh-lean explains; the book is a way to read what is proved, and from what.
