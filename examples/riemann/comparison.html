The question

The submission claims the Riemann hypothesis and the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, with a Lean proof of 175,187 modules and about a million declarations, without comments. There is no paper: the announcement says "It being obviously inappropriate to attempt to post some massive unreadable travesty of "slop", I decided to only present Lean" (p. 2). Comparator checks that the proof uses only the standard axioms and that the five theorems are those of Challenge.lean. What Lean cannot check is whether those statements say what they are meant to say, and, for understanding the proof, what its parts state. With no paper, the questions are:

  1. Are the claims the Riemann hypothesis and GRH? If they are, the result is proved, whatever the proof looks like (Section 1).
  2. What does the proof rest on? The step from the claims down to the statement that the proof proves (Section 2), and the three propositions from which the proof's last step derives it (Sections 3 and 4). These are where a reader can start to understand the argument, and to compare it with the announcement's outline and with the literature.

Reading. The five theorems state their propositions by name only (RiemannHypothesis, RiemannZetaZerosOnLine, …); their statements are read with these definitions unfolded, and the unfolded Lean is shown beside the source (#UNFOLD_STATEMENT of the exporter). The first is Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis, and the second states the same proposition word for word: every zero $s$ of $\zeta$ that is not $-2(n+1)$ and not $1$ has real part $\frac12$. The exception $s = 1$ is there because Mathlib's $\zeta$ has a value at its pole. The other three are GRH for Dirichlet L-functions: for every modulus $N$ and every Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $N$, imprimitive ones included, $L(\chi, s) \neq 0$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > \frac12$ (except $\chi = 1$ at $s = 1$), and the zeros with $0 < \operatorname{Re} s < 1$ are on the line $\frac12$. These are the standard statements, readable without Lean; the remarks of the submission's own verification notes (trivial zeros, $s = 1$, imprimitive characters, real $\frac12$) can all be checked on the readings. The announcement's "Extended Riemann Hypothesis" (for Dedekind zeta functions) is not claimed.
